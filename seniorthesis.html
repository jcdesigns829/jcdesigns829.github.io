Each piece breaks those marks apart and rebuilds them on a black ground, often fusing DC and Marvel icons in one frame. ---

Thesis outcomes

  1. Clarifying technical concerns

    The core question was a design one: how far can a logo be layered, recolored or redrawn before it stops reading? Combining photographs and rendered emblems with drawn shapes in Hero Emblems II and III also meant matching edges, scale and color.

  2. Personal growth and aesthetic awareness

    Working in series showed me what I keep returning to: iconic symbols, saturated color on black, and the pull between a precise logo and a loose mark. Lettering my studio name, JC Designs, above the Superman shield in Hero Emblems III ties the thesis to my own branding work.

  3. Alternate methodologies

    Each piece takes a different route: gestural strokes in the first, collage and texture in the second, typography and a grid in the third, and flat geometry with radiating line in the fourth.

  4. A method for new bodies of work

    The black ground and bright palette stay fixed while each piece changes which emblems meet and how they are rendered and arranged. It is a system I set for myself, and it extends to any family of symbols, not just superheroes.

  5. A resolved body of work

    Hero Emblems IV is where the series resolves: one subject, one radial composition and a tighter palette, with rings drawing the eye to the Iron Man helmet. It is the clearest version of my approach, giving iconic marks a designer's structure and a painter's hand.
